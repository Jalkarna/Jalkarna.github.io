About this text

The Manusmṛti presents itself as the teaching of Manu, the first man and lawgiver, recited to an assembly of sages by his pupil Bhṛgu. It moves from the creation of the world through the life of the student and householder, food and purity, the forest-dweller and renouncer, to the duties of the king, the conduct of lawsuits, inheritance, the mixed castes, penance, and finally action and rebirth.

It has been commented on, cited and contested for two thousand years, and was among the first Sanskrit works translated into European languages. Many of its rules, especially on caste and on women, are read today as historical evidence of a social order, not as guidance.
