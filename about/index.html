About Pothī

Pothī is a library of Sanskrit texts to be read whole: the Devanāgarī, its transliteration, a translation where a free and reliable one exists, and a recitation where one has been made. Every verse has its own address, so a reference can be shared as a link.

The name is the Hindi word for a bound manuscript: palm leaves or paper held on a cord between two boards.

How texts are edited

A text is listed only when all of its Sanskrit can be read here and its source allows republishing. There are no placeholders: a section of the library with nothing in it says so.

The Sanskrit is reproduced from a named electronic edition, and any change to it is recorded on the text's own page. Transliteration into IAST is generated from the Devanāgarī. Translations are published translations, credited and aligned verse by verse; where the alignment is not one to one, the text's page says where and why. Machine-written translations are not presented as authoritative.

Recitations may be synthetic. When they are, the text's page says so.

Licensing

Each text's page names its sources and their licences, and those terms carry over to what is shown here: a Sanskrit text taken from a Creative Commons source is republished under the same licence, with attribution, and a translation in the public domain is marked as such. Nothing is listed whose terms do not allow it.

The type is Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit by Tiro Typeworks and Literata by TypeTogether, both under the SIL Open Font License (Tiro, Literata).
